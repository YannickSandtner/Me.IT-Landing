The demographic challenge

A role can be refilled. Experience cannot be replaced overnight.

Retirement, succession and changing roles put tacit knowledge at risk. Documents explain the rule. Experienced colleagues know why an exception matters, when to ask for help and how to lead through ambiguity.

Experience leaves Onboarding slows Decisions become inconsistent Leadership context is lost

From knowledge to skills

Co-create the knowledge. Keep people accountable.

Me.IDs is a platform concept for employees and teams to co-design an Intellectual Twin: a transparent, bounded representation of professional knowledge—not a copy of a person. Experts capture cases, explain their reasoning and review what the twin may recommend.

01

Actor Twin

Co-design a professional knowledge profile with its owner: experience, reasoning patterns, limits and escalation routes. Make expertise accessible without impersonating its contributor.

02

Compound Knowledge Fabric

Connect approved cases, policy versions, decision rationales and exceptions. Keep sources, uncertainty, access rights and review dates visible as knowledge evolves.

03

Agentic Butler

Turn approved knowledge into repeatable support: learning scenarios, case briefings and handover checklists. Experts validate outputs before any consequential decision or action.

By design: human control

Agree participation and permitted use with employees and relevant stakeholders. Start with synthetic or de-identified cases, role-based access and reviewable sources. No autonomous coverage, pricing, claims or personnel decisions; no employee ranking.

Proposed use cases for insurance

Preserve judgement where insurance depends on it.

A proposal for discussion: explore how intellectual twins can preserve specialist judgement in the insurance industry.

01 · Composite

Composite underwriting and claims

Use cases to explore: object and hazard assessment in property, tariff classes and risk assessment in motor, and claims handling in property as well as liability and comprehensive motor claims.

Capture how experienced underwriters weigh protection measures, location and the particular characteristics of a risk against each other, how pricing actuaries classify driver, vehicle and regional classes and how claims specialists structure assessment steps and escalations.

The decision on acceptance, terms and payment always remains with the authorised individuals.

02 · Life

Medical risk and benefits assessment in life and disability

Use cases to explore: medical and occupational risk assessment in new business as well as benefits assessment in disability cases.

Capture which follow-up questions experienced risk assessors ask applicants, treating physicians and intermediaries, how they derive an overall assessment from pre-existing conditions, occupation profile and leisure-time risks and when they opt for a loading, an exclusion, postponement or referral to the reinsurer.

Also record how benefits assessors in disability cases determine the concrete occupation profile and the most recent activity, assess the degree of occupational disability and the prognosis period, weigh questions of occupational referral and re-examination and how they recognise when information is incomplete or unclear—for example as grounds for an expert opinion or for examining a breach of the pre-contractual duty of disclosure.

The assessment of medical matters and the final benefits decision always remain with the authorised individuals.

03 · Industrial

Underwriting and major-claims handling in industrial lines

Use cases to explore: risk assessment and wording design, structuring of international insurance programmes as well as settlement of major and complex losses.

Capture how experienced industrial underwriters condense type of operation, fire-protection concept, accumulation exposure and claims history into a PML estimate, how they derive conditions and deductibles from risk-engineering reports, where they draw the line in broker wordings and how they assemble capacity across layers, co-insurers and international programmes.

Also record how major-claims specialists separate coverage questions from the size of the loss, initiate expert assessment proceedings, verify business-interruption losses and steer advance payments, reserves and communication.

The decision on acceptance, terms, coverage and payment always remains with the authorised individuals.

04 · Cross-line

Succession and leadership

Use cases to explore: onboarding and succession handovers as well as passing on leadership knowledge.

Capture from approved cases, explanations and decision journals how experienced colleagues prepare typical cases and justify their assessment—and how leaders prioritise, escalate and decide under uncertainty. This results in a role-specific learning twin for onboarding and a reflection partner for day-to-day leadership.

Measure the benefit by the time to independent case preparation, the completeness of handovers and the quality of the scenarios played through.

Individual performance is explicitly not part of the assessment. Assessment and development of employees always remain with the leaders.

A proposed 90-day pilot

Start small. Learn together. Measure the difference.

01

Days 1–15: select one role and one knowledge-risk scenario. Agree participants, a baseline, permitted data and success criteria.

02

Days 16–40: co-create a small set of approved cases with experts and successors. Record reasoning, exceptions, sources and boundaries.

03

Days 41–70: test in a controlled environment against unseen scenarios. Check source accuracy, abstention, escalation and human review.

04

Days 71–90: compare results with the baseline. Decide together whether to refine, stop or expand. Benefits are hypotheses to test—not promised outcomes.
